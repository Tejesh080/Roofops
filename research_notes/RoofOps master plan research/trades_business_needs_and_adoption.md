## Key Question 1: Feature baselines and current AU pricing (ServiceM8, Tradify, Fergus, simPRO, AroFlo; AccuLynx, JobNimbus, Roofr for comparison)

### Takeaway
The AU small-trades baseline (ServiceM8/Tradify/Fergus) is: quote -> schedule -> mobile job card with photos -> invoice -> Xero sync, with SWMS/forms, purchase orders and timesheets gated to mid/upper tiers, and SMS charged per message or in bundles. Entry pricing is A$29/month flat (ServiceM8, unlimited users, job-capped) to A$48–80 per user/month (Tradify, Fergus, AroFlo); simPRO is quote-only. US roofing tools (Roofr, AccuLynx, JobNimbus) add aerial measurement, proposals/e-sign and production boards that AU trade tools do not have.

### Inferences
- A RoofOps feature "parity floor" against the AU small-trades market is: enquiry capture, quote with line items and e-acceptance, calendar/scheduling, mobile job card (check-in/out, photos, notes, materials/labour), forms incl. SWMS, purchase orders, progress/partial invoicing, invoice -> Xero on approval, per-message SMS for reminders and quote/invoice delivery, basic job costing. Everything beyond that (asset management, supplier catalogue integration, Gantt, proposals, AI helpers) is a differentiator the market gates behind higher tiers.
- Pricing-model pain is the strongest documented reason an owner would consider a custom build: per-job tiers (ServiceM8) or per-user tiers (Tradify/Fergus/AroFlo, 3-user minimum at AroFlo) scale against a growing roofing crew.
- Roofing-specific features that AU trade tools lack and US tools have (aerial measurement, proposal boards, production calendars) are candidates for RoofOps differentiation only if the business sells residential re-roofs at volume; for repair/maintenance work they are noise.

## Key Question 2: What owners actually use day-to-day, what they ignore, and where custom builds win or lose

### Takeaway
Across the review evidence that could be read (Xero App Store, Capterra, vendor help centres, contractor press), the features owners describe using are a short list: schedule view, mobile job card with photos/video, quote -> invoice, invoice push to Xero, and SMS to customers; complaints cluster on clunky mobile apps, missing small features, sync edge cases, and pricing models. No first-hand Reddit/Whirlpool threads could be retrieved, and no source compares custom-built systems with off-the-shelf tools for trades.

### Cited Findings
- Xero App Store ServiceM8 reviewers (4.6/5, 352 reviews, undated) say they value "the reports, photo and video capture, and sending invoices directly to Xero", that "onsite technicians can upload photos and videos from jobs", and that the web/app sync is "good". Frustrations: the iPhone app lacks features such as showing unconfirmed recurring bookings; typed notes on photos "can be glitchy and sometimes save as the word 'TEXT'". — [Xero App Store ServiceM8 reviews](https://apps.xero.com/au/industry/franchises/app/servicem8/reviews)
- Xero App Store Tradify reviewers (4.6/5, 187 reviews) cite the scheduler making "it clear what's been done and what's still on", two-way Xero sync that "Cuts down on time so much", and that "his accountant can see what she needs without seeing his whole diary". - Capterra ServiceM8 reviews (mostly 2016–2017, so dated): "a checklist for technicians' iPads had effectively replaced paper work orders"; complaints were lack of training videos, a search function that "doesn't always work", and wanting a card surcharge on invoices. — [Capterra ServiceM8 reviews p.4](https://www.capterra.com/p/110711/ServiceM8/reviews/?page=4)

### Inferences
- The "daily core" that staff describe using is five things: today's schedule, the job card (photos, notes, materials), quote/invoice creation, invoice -> Xero, and customer SMS. Reports and job costing are owner/office features used weekly, not daily.
- A custom build wins where the pricing model (per job or per user) is punitive for a growing crew, where the workflow is roofing-specific (SWMS per site visit, compliance certificate issue, progress claims, roof-specific quote templates), and where device mix (Android + iOS) matters. It loses where years of edge-case handling exist in the commercial Xero integrations (invoice numbering clashes, archived contacts, zero-value invoices), supplier integrations, and form libraries, and where no vendor support/partner network exists when the solo engineer is unavailable.

## Key Question 3: Rollout and adoption practices for tradespeople (pilot, parallel run, mobile-first, who enters data, time to trust, failure stories)

### Takeaway
The documented pattern is: one owner/office "subject matter expert" learns the system first; a single pilot crew chosen for influence runs real jobs with the old process still available; a hard go-live date (ServiceM8 recommends a Monday) after which all new jobs go into the new system and the old one is retired; and 30/60/90-day reviews. The most-cited failure modes are a parallel spreadsheet that never retires, a clunky multi-tap mobile flow, login/connectivity friction, and training used as a substitute for fit.

### Cited Findings

**Vendor-documented go-live practice**
- ServiceM8's official go-live checklist (21 items) requires each staff member to have a login, security roles set ("Do you want staff to be able see pricing?", "...see all jobs?", "...create jobs?", "...delete clients?"), staff to complete "Levels 1 & 2 of the Learning Roadmap", and the field minimum: "How to view all jobs assigned to them?", "How to Start a job & Navigate to it?", "How to Check In and Check Out of jobs?", "How to take & save photos to a job?", "How to add labour & materials to a job?", "How to complete a job, record payment, and issue an invoice?", "How to Clock On/Off". It says to choose a go-live date, ideally a Monday; from that date use ServiceM8 for all jobs; until then keep using the existing system and only enter older jobs if there is a real need; then clear dummy data with the Account Cleanup Wizard. — [ServiceM8: Checklist — are you ready to go live](https://support.servicem8.com/help-center/desktop/basics/checklist-are-you-ready-to-go-live)
- ServiceM8's Learning Roadmap is self-paced short videos; "Field staff get the basics, while operations staff get the basics plus advanced features". — [How to train staff with the Learning Roadmap](https://support.servicem8.com/help-center/desktop/basics/how-to-train-staff-with-the-learning-roadmap)
- Fergus: designate one subject matter expert ("could be the owner") and "give them the time and support they need to learn all aspects of the system"; that person trains the team. Customer quote: "you should implement the system before there's even a real requirement for it" (Jim, JMTEC Services) because low job volume gives time to practise. — [Fergus: the trades guide to job management software](https://fergus.com/tradehub/blog/the-trades-guide-to-job-management-software/)
- Tradify runs a 45-minute guided migration webinar covering "the dashboard, turning enquiries into jobs, scheduling, quoting, and invoicing". — [Tradify migrate webinar](https://www.tradifyhq.com/webinar-migrate)

**Pilot and parallel-run guidance**
- A 90-day field-service plan: weeks 8–9 pilot = "One crew, one service line, real jobs, with the old process still available"; pilot crew chosen "for influence, not convenience" (the technician others go to for advice); pilot-exit tests: a full day of jobs ran on the app with no paper fallback; invoices generated from work orders, not retyped; accounting integration posted invoices and returned payments; "No technician records the same information twice"; the crew, asked directly, would choose the new system. Switch-off date set at kickoff, owned by the internal champion, executed "on a date" not gradually; two weeks of hypercare through the first billing cycle; vendor claims "90%+ adoption within 90 days". — [MangoApps: field service implementation first 90 days](https://www.mangoapps.com/articles/field-service-implementation-first-90-days)
- AU adviser (ScaleSuite) 90-day plan in 30-day phases: "Make field time capture mandatory for payroll and job cost on a pilot crew only"; "Expand the tool to remaining crews only after data quality is real"; "Configure job cost categories to match your chart of accounts before the first live job"; "Reconcile job tool invoices and costs to Xero every week at first, then at least monthly"; owner reviews WIP and margin weekly for the first three months; "Budget training and a process owner; the tool will not adopt itself"; "Expect process discipline before margin lift, not magic in week one"; "dual systems kill accuracy". — [ScaleSuite best job management software trades AU](https://www.scalesuite.com.au/resources/best-job-management-software-trades)
- Phased rollout "starting with one team or office, to reduce disruption"; set measurable goals ("reduce scheduling time from 2 hours to 30 minutes per day"); "A typical implementation takes 4-8 weeks" (Oct 2026 article, generic field workforce). — [yourradar: how to implement workforce management software (3 Oct 2026)](https://www.yourradar.net/2026/10/03/how-to-implement-workforce-management-software-step-by-step/)
- Rollout team should include "field workers, back-office staff, and contractors"; "Encourage feedback and address any questions or concerns"; clean data first ("Remove duplicates and standardize essential fields before importing"). — [WorkBuddy: 7-step guide to implementing job management software](https://workbuddy.com/blog/implementing-job-management-software/)
- Attribution unverified: a search snippet among the above results stated "Run new jobs in the new system only. Let existing jobs finish the old way. Running everything in parallel doubles the work and kills the rollout" and "Start with quoting, not the crew. The office feels the benefit first, and a platform that is already producing quotes is easier to sell to the van" with "A Practical Four-Week Software Pilot". The exact page could not be confirmed (not on the ScaleSuite or Fergus pages fetched; likely one of [buildern](https://buildern.com/resources/blog/trade-job-management-software/) or [needtoknowai](https://www.needtoknowai.com/guides/best-job-management-software-for-trades/)).

**Failure stories and causes**
- Technicians rarely reject technology per se; tools were "designed for dispatchers and office staff, and the mobile version shows it"; apps that need a live connection fit "offices with good WiFi, not industrial sites"; technicians asked to use "a login they don't remember, a signal they don't have, and an email address they were never given" fall back to paper or memory, "and that habit persists even after connectivity improves"; needing "three separate apps per job" is a major barrier. — [Prometheus Group: why field technicians resist mobile apps](https://www.prometheusgroup.com/learning-center/why-field-technicians-resist-mobile-maintenance-apps-prometheus-group)
- "If a system feels slow, adds extra taps, or interrupts familiar routines, it gets ignored"; the spreadsheet "never goes away because nobody set a date for it to go, and the parallel process becomes the real one". — [MangoApps: why technicians work around field service software](https://www.mangoapps.com/articles/why-technicians-work-around-field-service-software)
- Failures "are rarely for technical reasons. The two causes are unowned decisions and a technician who doesn't use the app"; when technicians don't use the app "the office keeps the old process running, creating two records". — [MangoApps first 90 days](https://www.mangoapps.com/articles/field-service-implementation-first-90-days)
- "the 70% failure figure is the default outcome when change management is treated as an afterthought to the software purchase" — the 70% figure is repeated without a primary source. — [Technology.org field service software implementation plan (15 Oct 2026)](https://www.technology.org/2026/10/15/field-service-software-implementation-plan/); [fieldservicesoftware.io FSM change management](https://fieldservicesoftware.io/blog/fsm-change-management-tips/)
- Panorama Consulting: failures are "less to do with software features than executives might think"; causes include inconsistent dispatch rules and success metrics focused on go-live timing rather than usage. — [Panorama: why FSM systems fail](https://www.panorama-consulting.com/why-field-service-management-systems-fail-and-how-to-avoid-it/)
- Fit before training: "Training alone rarely works because it assumes the app already fits the work." — [Oxmaint: why technicians hate your software](https://oxmaint.com/industries/property-management/maintenance-technicians-hate-current-software-how-to-fix)
- Enforcement example of what paper processes miss: Proform Roofing (Vic) was found guilty on 12 Feb 2026 of nine offences and fined $700,000 after inspectors found roof workers at 2.9 m, 3.2 m and 3 m with no fall protection and "No SWMS had been prepared" / "No SWMS was available" at three sites in 2023–2024. — [WorkSafe Victoria, 20 Feb 2026](https://www.worksafe.vic.gov.au/news/2026-02/700000-fines-string-working-height-offences)

### Inferences
- For RoofOps, a defensible rollout is: (1) owner/office uses enquiry -> quote -> invoice -> Xero for 2–4 weeks alone (office benefit first, no crew dependency); (2) one influential crew runs the mobile job card on real jobs for 2 weeks with paper still permitted; (3) a Monday go-live after which every new job is created in RoofOps and the old spreadsheet/diary is read-only; (4) weekly Xero reconciliation and owner WIP review for 90 days; (5) adoption judged by "no double entry" and "no paper fallback for a full day", not by logins.
- Data entry ownership should be explicit per step: office creates enquiry/quote/job and approves invoices; crew lead only does check-in, photos, SWMS sign-on, materials used and "complete"; nobody retypes anything.
- "Time until trust" is not measured in any source; the proxies (4–8 weeks implementation, 90 days to 90%+ adoption, three months of weekly owner review) suggest planning for one full quarter before relying on RoofOps data for margin decisions.

### Gaps
- No Australian accountant/bookkeeper or vendor case study describing a trades rollout timeline with before/after metrics was found (vendor customer stories found are testimonial-style, e.g. Fergus/King Spark Electrical).
- No source quantifies how long until field staff "trust" a system; the 90%-in-90-days figure is a vendor claim.
- The "run new jobs only / start with quoting" snippet is unattributed (see above).

## Key Question 4: Australian compliance touchpoints (SWMS/HRCW, compliance certificates by state, ATO tax invoices, Spam Act and ACMA SMS enforcement, Sender ID Register)

### Takeaway
A roofing system must (a) force a SWMS before any work where a fall of more than 2 m is possible and keep it accessible on site; (b) in Victoria, generate a VBA compliance certificate for roof plumbing work worth $750 or more (incl. GST, labour, materials, fixtures) within 5 days of completion, and in NSW record the roof plumbing licence and any Certificate of Compliance; (c) issue ATO-compliant tax invoices (words "tax invoice", ABN, date, description, GST, and buyer identity/ABN at $1,000+); and (d) send marketing SMS only with consent, with sender identification and a working no-login unsubscribe honoured within 5 working days, from a registered sender ID after 1 July 2026. ACMA penalties in 2025–2026 ran from $75,600 (Oneflare, a tradie lead marketplace) to $4,003,270 (Tabcorp).

### Cited Findings

**SWMS / high-risk construction work**
- Under the model WHS framework, roof work is high risk construction work (HRCW) where a person could fall more than 2 metres; a SWMS "must be prepared before work is commenced". Falls "are the major cause of death and injury when working on roofs". — [Safe Work Australia: Safe work on roofs information sheet (docx)](https://safeworkaustralia.gov.au/system/files/documents/1702/safe-work-roofs-information-sheet.docx); [Safe Work Australia duties tool: working at heights](https://safeworkaustralia.gov.au/duties-tool/construction/hazards-information/working-heights)
- The SWA SWMS information sheet: SWMS are required for the 18 HRCW activities defined in the WHS Regulations; "a SWMS is not required for 'work of a minor nature'". — [SWA: SWMS for HRCW information sheet](https://safeworkaustralia.gov.au/doc/safe-work-method-statement-high-risk-construction-work-information-sheet); [WorkSafe WA: SWMS for HRCW](https://worksafe.wa.gov.au/publications/safe-work-method-statements-high-risk-construction-work)
- SafeWork SA: duty holders "must ensure SWMS are prepared and accessible to persons who need it prior to works commencing" and a SWMS "must be periodically reviewed to ensure that at all times it accurately reflects the safe work method"; PCBUs "must always manage fall hazards – even below two metres". — [SA DPTI SWMS fact sheet](https://www.dpti.sa.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0011/571727/DOCS_AND_FILES-13976017-v1-Safe_Work_Method_Statement_Fact_Sheet.pdf); [SafeWork SA roofing PDF](https://www.safework.sa.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/1386831/373088fd5e68b37761e2fb60221c140a9ddebdac.pdf)
- South Australia's HRCW fall threshold moves from 3 m to 2 m from 1 July 2026, aligning with other states. — [Climate Control News: SA amends safety framework](https://www.climatecontrolnews.com.au/air-conditioning/sa-amends-safety-framework)
- Victoria (OHS Regulations, not model WHS) enforces the same 2 m SWMS requirement: WorkSafe guidance quoted in the Proform case: "Ensure HRCW is not performed unless a Safe Work Method Statement (SWMS) is prepared." — [WorkSafe Victoria, 20 Feb 2026](https://www.worksafe.vic.gov.au/news/2026-02/700000-fines-string-working-height-offences)
- Industry guidance: review SWMS "regularly and ... make revisions if work processes or control measures change". — [Master Builders Queensland: SWMS](https://www.mbqld.com.au/resource-hub/health-safety-and-environment/safety-documents-and-signage/safe-work-method-statements)

**Compliance certificates — Victoria**
- VBA: a compliance certificate must be issued when the total value of plumbing work is $750 or more; the total "includes the cost of labour, materials, appliances, fixtures (regardless of who purchased them) and GST"; the certificate must be given to the customer within five days of completing the work; regulated plumbing work is covered by insurance for six years under a compliance certificate; some work needs a certificate regardless of value (e.g. below-ground sanitary drains). — [VBA PES Compliance Certificates — Obligations and Common Enquiries (PDF)](https://www.vba.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/165690/PES-Compliance-Certificates-Obligations-and-Common-Enquiries.pdf); [VBA fact sheet PES 16 May 2024 common plumbing enquiries](https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0011/25013/VBA-Fact-Sheet-PES-16-May-2024-Common-Plumbing-Enquiries.pdf)
- Roofing is regulated plumbing work in Victoria: "all roofing such as metallic or polycarbonate roofing, including eaves and box gutters" must be installed by a registered or licensed plumber who issues a compliance certificate; a building owner doing roofing on their own house without a certificate "is not permitted". — [Plumbing Connection: compliance certificates protect your work](https://plumbingconnection.com.au/plumbing-compliance-certificates-protect-your-work/); [VBA: plumbers pay the price for breaking the rules (2021)](https://www.vba.vic.gov.au/news/news/2021/plumbers-pay-the-price-for-breaking-the-rules)

**Compliance — NSW**
- A licence is required before doing residential roof plumbing "valued at more than $5000 (incl GST) in labour and materials"; roof plumbing covers "guttering, downpipes, roof flashing and roof coverings" and excludes non-metallic tiles/slates, glass, concrete, timber, thatching and bituminous membranes. — [NSW Government: roof plumbing work](https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/licences-and-credentials/building-and-trade-licences-and-registrations/roof-plumbing-work)
- Plumbers and drainers "must complete a CoC at the completion of all plumbing and drainage work", give a copy to whoever arranged the work and submit it to Building Commission NSW; exemptions listed are emergency work and minor works. — [NSW Government: plumbing inspection documents](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/compliance-and-regulation/plumbers-and-drainers/plumbing-inspection-documents)

**ATO tax invoice requirements**
- A tax invoice is required when "a taxable sale is more than $82.50 (including GST)" or when "a customer asks for one (regardless of the amount) – you have 28 days to give it to them". Required fields: the words "tax invoice"; business name; ABN; date issued; a short description of what was sold including quantity and price; the GST amount payable; and "You must also include the buyer's identity or ABN on invoices for sales over $1000." — [business.gov.au: how to invoice](https://business.gov.au/finance/payments-and-invoicing/how-to-invoice)
- GST may be shown per item, or where GST is exactly one-eleventh of the total, a statement such as "Total price includes GST" is acceptable. — [RMIT RUSU Australian tax invoice requirements (Mar 2025)](https://rusu.rmit.edu.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/4_Australian-Tax-Invoice-Requirements.pdf); [Xero AU tax invoice requirements guide](https://www.xero.com/au/guides/tax-invoice-requirements/)

**Spam Act 2003 and ACMA enforcement (SMS/email)**
- Unsubscribe facility rules as stated by Clayton Utz (2 Apr 2025): "has clear instructions on how to unsubscribe (or 'opt-out' or 'stop')"; "is actioned within 5 working days"; "does not charge a fee"; "works for at least 30 days after the message was sent"; "does not require the recipient to create or log in to an account" or "provide personal information (other than the number or email address to which the message was sent)". — [Clayton Utz: ACMA enforcement action — unsubscribe rules](https://www.claytonutz.com/insights/2025/april/acma-enforcement-action-do-your-marketing-communications-comply-with-the-spam-acts-unsubscribe-rules)
- Telstra: $626,000 infringement notice (the maximum available by notice at the time) after "close to 10.5 million texts" of which over 10.3 million had a non-compliant unsubscribe; ACMA held that "requiring the recipient to enter a PIN was equivalent to requesting the recipient to log into an account". — [Clayton Utz](https://www.claytonutz.com/insights/2025/april/acma-enforcement-action-do-your-marketing-communications-comply-with-the-spam-acts-unsubscribe-rules)
- Tabcorp: ACMA penalty of $4,003,270 (April 2025) over roughly 5,700 messages to VIP customers, including ~2,500 SMS/WhatsApp without a working unsubscribe, 3,148 with inadequate sender information and 11 SMS without consent; plus a three-year court-enforceable undertaking with quarterly audits. — [Mondaq: the Spam Act — an update](https://www.mondaq.com/australia/compliance/1667464/the-spam-act-an-update); [iGB: Tabcorp penalty for spamming VIP customers](https://igamingbusiness.com/legal-compliance/tabcorp-penalty-spamming-vip-customers/)
- Lululemon: $702,900 infringement notice for contravening s18(1) (no functional unsubscribe) in more than 370,000 commercial emails (not SMS) sent 1 Dec 2024–5 Jan 2025; announced 11 Mar 2026; plus a court-enforceable undertaking. — [ACMA Lululemon infringement notice (redacted PDF, Mar 2026)](https://www.acma.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-03/Lululemon%20-%20Spam%20Infringement%20Notice_Redacted.pdf); [Mediaweek: Lululemon pays $702,900](https://www.mediaweek.com.au/lululemon-pays-702900-fine-after-370000-emails-breach-spam-act)
- Oneflare (tradie lead marketplace): $75,600 infringement notice for SMS sent to numbers taken from public directories and messages lacking an unsubscribe option, plus an enforceable undertaking (undated in source; believed pre-2024 — date unverified). — [iTWire: Oneflare cops $75,000 fine](https://itwire.com/business-it-news/security/oneflare-cops-$75,000-fine-over-spam-rule-breaches)
- ACMA's position: a mostly factual (service) message that also contains marketing content, or links to it, needs consent. — [Mediaweek: Latitude Finance $3.96m for 2.7m spam texts](https://mediaweek.com.au/news/latitude-finance-pays-3-96m-penalty-for-2-7m-spam-texts)
- Other reference points: Ticketek $515,040 (2023) for ~98,000 texts/emails to unsubscribed customers; Kmart $1,303,500 for 212,471 emails to unsubscribed customers (date not given in source). — [HSF Kramer: Ticketek (Oct 2023)](https://www.hsfkramer.com/insights/2023-10/unsubscribe-means-unsubscribe-ticketek-pays-over-500000-for-breaching-spam-act); [Lexology: spam emails must have an unsubscribe link](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=69db4b9e-c8e7-43f4-b26f-39f88a8818e2)
- Alphanumeric sender IDs usually cannot receive replies, so a "Reply STOP" opt-out can be unworkable; use a number that can receive replies or a link-based opt-out. — [Telerivet: Australia SMS compliance](https://www.telerivet.com/blog/australia-sms-compliance-acma-sender-id-spam-act)

**SMS Sender ID Register**
- "From 1 July 2026, branded sender IDs must be registered with the SMS Sender ID Register"; unregistered branded (alphanumeric) sender IDs are shown as "Unverified" and may be grouped with potential scam messages; registration is tied to the business entity "often via ABN details"; "Registration of a sender ID does not remove or reduce" Spam Act obligations (consent, identification, functional unsubscribe). — [HWL Ebsworth: identity crisis — new ACMA rules (8 May 2026)](https://hwlebsworth.com.au/identity-crisis-new-acma-rules-for-sms-communications-and-the-sender-id-register/); [Nexon: Sender ID Register requirements from 1 July 2026](https://nexon.com.au/news/new-sms-sender-id-register-requirements-take-effect-from-1-july-2026/)
- Registration is done through the telco or SMS platform, which submits to the register; an ABN is required; an earlier proposal had a 15 Dec 2025 start, later moved. — [Aussie Broadband: SMS Sender ID Register](https://www.aussiebroadband.com.au/help-centre/mobile/what-you-need-to-know-about-the-sms-sender-id-register/); [Lexer (19 May 2025)](https://www.lexer.io/blog/be-seen-not-screened-new-anti-scam-sms-regulations)

### Inferences
- RoofOps job model needs: a per-job "fall risk > 2 m" flag that blocks "start work" until a SWMS is attached and signed by the crew on that site visit; SWMS versioning with a review trigger when the method changes; a state field that drives the compliance-certificate rule (VIC: value >= $750 incl. GST -> certificate due within 5 days of completion; NSW: licence number captured on jobs > $5,000, CoC where plumbing/drainage work is involved).
- Invoice templates must hard-code "Tax Invoice", ABN, date, line descriptions with quantity/price, GST amount, and require customer name/ABN when total >= $1,000 (business.gov.au says "over $1000"; treat $1,000 as the trigger).
- For RoofOps Lite: SMS to an enquirer who asked for a quote is arguably a service message, but any upsell or promotional content makes it commercial; the safe design is to record consent at enquiry capture, identify the business by name in every message, include "Reply STOP" from a reply-capable number (or a link), suppress within 5 working days, keep the opt-out live for 30 days, and register the sender ID (via Twilio) before 1 July 2026 deadlines (already passed as of research date).

### Gaps
- ACMA primary pages (acma.gov.au/investigations-spam-and-telemarketing, /avoid-sending-spam, /sms-sender-id-register) timed out; enforcement details come from law-firm and press summaries plus one ACMA PDF. The name of the ACMA industry standard establishing the register was not confirmed.
- SWMS retention period (model WHS Reg 303 is understood to require keeping the SWMS until the HRCW is completed, or 2 years if a notifiable incident occurs) could not be verified from a primary page in this session — mark as "to verify".
- Whether roof plumbing on its own (sheeting, flashing, gutters without stormwater connection) triggers a NSW Certificate of Compliance is unresolved; sources only confirm the licence threshold and the general CoC rule for plumbing and drainage work.
- Queensland (QBCC) and WA roofing certificate requirements were not researched.
- The ATO page itself returned 403; business.gov.au wording was used instead.

## Key Question 5: What accountants/bookkeepers expect from a Xero integration and common complaints

### Takeaway
The expectations evidenced in vendor help centres and integration guides are: push invoices only once approved (never zero-value or half-finished), never reuse or collide invoice numbers, match contacts deterministically (by stored ContactID/email, not name) and handle archived contacts, write tracking categories that map to the chart of accounts before the first live job, and make every write idempotent so retries never duplicate. Draft-status pushes shift cleanup onto the bookkeeper.

### Cited Findings
- Xero API invoice states as summarised by an integration guide: DRAFT is editable and "not committed to the ledger, with no required-field strictness"; SUBMITTED means waiting on a reviewer/approval app; AUTHORISED means approved and ready for payment; incomplete data that would silently land as a DRAFT "will fail at the write call if you try to post it as AUTHORISED", so a common pattern is to write as DRAFT then transition so validation errors surface. — [invoicedataextraction: Xero invoice status AUTHORISED](https://invoicedataextraction.com/blog/xero-invoice-status-authorised); [Apideck: invoice status values](https://help.apideck.com/en/articles/8784898)
- Xero's duplicate detection does not cover items awaiting approval: a user reports several staff loaded the same bill and Xero "did not flag the third one because the other two were still awaiting approval". — [Xero product ideas: auto-approve smart capture](https://productideas.xero.com/forums/967130-contacts-documents/suggestions/51312658-smart-document-capture-ability-to-auto-approve-t)
- Draft invoices pushed from Xero Projects show as "invoiced", which users complain misrepresents the job state. — [Xero product ideas: draft invoices should not appear as invoiced](https://productideas.xero.com/forums/967124-projects-tracking/suggestions/51408544-draft-invoices-should-not-appear-as-invoiced-in)
- Contact matching: "Contacts in Xero lack strict uniqueness rules", so "Smith & Associates" and "Smith and Associates" can coexist, and Xero "silently creates a new contact record" when an imported name doesn't match exactly; guidance is "Always search Xero Contacts first — by email address, not name." — [dataclean: clean duplicate contacts from Xero](https://dataclean.to/use-cases/clean-duplicates-from-xero); [GoHighLevel Xero integration guide (2026)](https://hlgrowthpartner.com/post/gohighlevel-xero-integration-setup-cost-2026)
- Idempotency: "store the GHL invoice ID against the created Xero InvoiceID and check it before creating. Webhooks get replayed and retries happen". — [GoHighLevel Xero integration guide](https://hlgrowthpartner.com/post/gohighlevel-xero-integration-setup-cost-2026)
- Poorly designed integrations "can create messy data ... for example by creating duplicate contacts or posting to the wrong accounts"; if a job system raises invoices without the correct tracking category, "your bookkeeper ends up fixing every invoice by hand". — [Kenomont: hiring a Xero developer](https://kenomont.com/blog/hiring-xero-developer-custom-api-integration); [LedgerUp: automate contract-to-cash in Xero](https://www.ledgerup.ai/resources/how-to-automate-contract-to-cash-in-xero)
- Xero's Copy & Merge "creates a new draft and leaves the originals in place", which risks double-billing. — [datasights: consolidated invoicing in Xero](https://datasights.co/consolidated-invoicing-in-xero/)
- Commercial-product behaviour that sets bookkeeper expectations: ServiceM8 sends invoices only on approval, never zero-value invoices, creates the Xero contact only with the first approved invoice, errors on archived contacts and on duplicate invoice numbers ("Searching Xero with deleted and voided invoices included is the usual first check"). — [ServiceM8 Xero sync](https://support.servicem8.com/hc/en-us/articles/204753684-How-the-sync-with-Xero-works); [ServiceM8 invoice number conflicts](https://support.servicem8.com/help-center/servicem8-add-ons/xero/resolving-xero-invoice-number-conflicts); [Tradify invoice status error](https://help.tradifyhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/22705652224537-Xero-Error-Invoice-not-of-valid-status-for-modification)
- AU adviser: "Configure job cost categories to match your chart of accounts before the first live job"; "Reconcile job tool invoices and costs to Xero every week at first, then at least monthly". — [ScaleSuite](https://www.scalesuite.com.au/resources/best-job-management-software-trades)
- What a good integration looks like to a tradie's accountant: "his accountant can see what she needs without seeing his whole diary" (Tradify Xero review). — [Xero App Store Tradify reviews](https://apps.xero.com/au/app/tradify/reviews)

### Inferences
- RoofOps should: store Xero ContactID on the customer and Xero InvoiceID on the invoice; search by email/ABN before creating contacts; use an idempotency key per invoice push; push as AUTHORISED only on explicit office approval (optionally DRAFT for a "review in Xero" mode the bookkeeper can choose); reserve invoice numbers from a single sequence and check Xero (including voided/deleted) before use; write the job/site tracking category and a fixed sales account per line; never push $0 invoices; surface sync errors on a dashboard the office sees daily.
- Bookkeepers' real complaint is cleanup labour; a weekly "RoofOps vs Xero" reconciliation report (count and total of invoices by status) directly addresses it.

### Gaps
- Official Xero developer documentation text (invoice status transitions, Idempotency-Key header, LineItem Tracking max 2) could not be captured: developer.xero.com renders client-side and the Xero-OpenAPI YAML (932k chars) sections fetched at offsets 0/350k/850k did not contain the Invoice schema. Verify against https://developer.xero.com/documentation/api/accounting/invoices before implementation.
- No bookkeeper forum/survey evidence specifically naming ServiceM8/Tradify/simPRO Xero data quality was found; complaints above are from vendor help centres and consultant blogs.

## Key Question 6: How long until a custom operations system is in daily use, and what "minimum viable adoption" looks like

### Takeaway
No source measures time-to-daily-use for a custom-built trades system; the nearest proxies are 4–8 weeks for a commercial implementation, an 8–12-week pilot with cutover inside 90 days, and a vendor claim of 90%+ adoption by day 90. The commercial go-live checklists and AU adviser guidance converge on a daily minimum of five flows: office enquiry -> quote, schedule board, field job card (check-in, photos, materials, complete), invoice approve -> Xero, and customer SMS.

### Cited Findings
- "A typical implementation takes 4-8 weeks" (generic field-workforce software, Oct 2026). — [yourradar (3 Oct 2026)](https://www.yourradar.net/2026/10/03/how-to-implement-workforce-management-software-step-by-step/)
- 8–12-week pilot with cutover inside 90 days; "90%+ adoption within 90 days" (vendor claim). — [MangoApps first 90 days](https://www.mangoapps.com/articles/field-service-implementation-first-90-days)
- 90-day plan in 30-day phases with weekly owner review of WIP/margin for three months; light tools should prioritise "mobile time capture, simple quoting and Xero invoice sync". — [ScaleSuite](https://www.scalesuite.com.au/resources/best-job-management-software-trades)
- ServiceM8's field minimum for go-live: view assigned jobs, create/schedule (incl. urgent and rescheduling), start and navigate, check in/out, photos, labour and materials, complete/record payment/issue invoice, clock on/off; office minimum: job statuses, scheduling in dashboard and app, unscheduled-jobs list, billable items -> quote/invoice, send invoices by email and SMS, accounting connection. — [ServiceM8 go-live checklist](https://support.servicem8.com/help-center/desktop/basics/checklist-are-you-ready-to-go-live)
- Tradify's migration session defines the core loop as "the dashboard, turning enquiries into jobs, scheduling, quoting, and invoicing". — [Tradify migrate webinar](https://www.tradifyhq.com/webinar-migrate)
- "implement the system before there's even a real requirement for it" so staff practise on low volume. — [Fergus trades guide](https://fergus.com/tradehub/blog/the-trades-guide-to-job-management-software/)
- Pilot-exit criteria usable as adoption KPIs: a full day of jobs with no paper fallback; invoices generated from work orders not retyped; integration posted invoices and returned payments; no double entry; crew prefers the new system. — [MangoApps first 90 days](https://www.mangoapps.com/articles/field-service-implementation-first-90-days)

### Inferences
- Minimum viable adoption for RoofOps (five screens): (1) Office "Enquiries & Quotes" (create enquiry, build quote, send, mark accepted -> job); (2) "Schedule" board (assign crew/date); (3) Field "Today" list + "Job card" (check-in, SWMS sign-on when fall-risk flagged, photos, materials used, complete); (4) Office "Invoices awaiting approval" (approve -> Xero); (5) "Messages" (SMS quote/appointment reminders, Lite lead follow-up with consent and STOP). Purchase orders, job costing, reports and copilot features are week-2+ or owner-only.
- Daily-use measures: new jobs created in RoofOps / all jobs started (target 100% after go-live Monday); job cards completed in-app the same day (target >90% by day 30); invoices pushed to Xero within 1 business day of completion; zero invoices retyped in Xero; SWMS attached on 100% of fall-risk jobs.
- A realistic calendar for a solo engineer and a small crew: weeks 1–3 office-only (quotes/invoices/Xero), weeks 4–5 one crew pilot, week 6 Monday go-live for all new jobs, weeks 6–18 weekly reconciliation and owner review; expect trust in the numbers around the end of the first quarter.

### Gaps
- No evidence on custom (in-house) systems specifically; all timelines are for commercial products and most are vendor-authored.
- No AU study of adoption rates by trade or crew size.
